# Configuration: project agents, runners, anchor-hub, router, auth and client

What this page covers: the config keys for project agents, steps, runners, the item model, the Machines panel, gbrain and second brains, anchor-hub, the pilot and router, the For you list, the portal, sign-in and the client. Back to the [README](../README.md).

The keys are grouped in three pages by what they set: [Configuration](configuration.md), [Configuration: project agents, runners, anchor-hub, router, auth and client](configuration-integrations.md) and [Configuration: keywords for another language](configuration-keywords.md). Each has the same four columns.

| `project_agent.form_senders` | `[]` | The `--sender` labels of the fleet tool's New session form (sala's: `["sala-web"]`; empty = the form is off). ## Gmail thread anchors: the link

A Gmail thread is added by its link, in either form: the browser's `https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/<thread id>` or the one the Workspace connector returns, `https://mail.google.com/mail/u/<address>/#all/<thread id>`. After `/u/` comes a number or an address: one local part (letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `-` and a plus, written as `+` or `%2B`), then `@` (or `%40`), then a dotted domain such as `example.org`. The view after `#` may be `all`, `inbox`, `sent`, `label/<name>` or `search/<terms>`, with no empty part; the thread id is the 16 hex digits at the end. Every form of one thread is the same anchor (`gmail:<thread id>`): a second form of a thread a project already holds adds nothing, and removing either form removes it. A link that is none of these (no `#` view, an empty part in the view, a thread id of another length, a scheme other than `https` or `http`, an account that is neither a number nor an address of that shape) is refused when added. A link already stored with an empty part in its view (for example `#inbox//<thread id>`, which an earlier version read as the thread) is no longer read as one: its URL text is kept as stored and it has no thread anchor id.

Two entries already stored for one thread on one project's page (say `#inbox/<thread id>` for one item and `#all/<thread id>` for another) now behave as one anchor, because they are one: a removal by either form removes both, the lists of the items they name read it once (each item still sees the URL of its own entry), and adding a form of it with other items widens the first entry. The store keeps the URL text as it was typed.

## Gmail query anchors: the query

An anchor `gmail:q:<query>` (every mailbox the anchors service reads) or `gmail:q@<mailbox>[,<mailbox>]:<query>` (only those) tells a project's agents about each new mail whose headers match the query. The mailbox scope is described under `anchor_hub.gmail_names` above; this section is the query.

**Terms.** A term is `key:value` with a key of `from`, `to`, `cc`, `subject` or `person` (`person` is from, to or cc), or a bare value, which is a subject term. A value is a quoted phrase or the words up to the next key, operator or parenthesis, so `subject:anchor test person:sam@example.com` is two terms and `subject:quarterly numbers` looks for the phrase "quarterly numbers". A term matches when the header contains the value, in any case. Keys are lowercase.

**Combining terms.**

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `from:a subject:b` or `from:a AND subject:b` | both must match (a space is an AND wherever a key, a quoted phrase or a parenthesis starts a new term; plain words run together are one phrase) |
| `from:a OR from:b` | either |
| `( ... )` | grouping |
| `from:(a OR b)` | the key applies to every term in the group: the same as `from:a OR from:b` (no other key may appear inside) |
| `"a b"` | a quoted phrase, for text that holds a parenthesis, a colon, a quotation mark or the words OR and AND |

AND binds tighter than OR: `from:a OR from:b subject:x` is `from:a OR (from:b AND subject:x)`. Write parentheses when it matters; the add reply shows how the query was read. `OR` and `AND` are operators only in capitals (Gmail's rule); `or` and `and` are ordinary words. There is no negation (a leading `-`) and no other Gmail operator (`label:`, `has:`, `after:`, `in:`, ...); `{a b}` is not supported either, write `a OR b`.

**An unsupported query is refused when it is added**, with one line naming what is wrong, and nothing is stored: a parenthesis or quotation mark that is never closed, a closing parenthesis with no opening one, `OR` or `AND` with nothing on one side, empty parentheses or an empty quoted phrase, a key with no value, a key inside `key:( )`, negation, `{ }`, a Gmail operator the matcher cannot read, any other `word:value` that is not one of the five keys (put it in quotation marks to match it as text), and a parenthesis or quotation mark glued to a word. An anchor stored before the grammar whose text the grammar refuses keeps the meaning it always had (every term must match, a value runs to the next key). An anchor stored before the grammar that the grammar now accepts is read by the grammar, so it can read differently than before: `Terms OR Conditions update` was one phrase and is now an OR, and the parentheses of `subject:invoice (urgent)` were literal text and are now a group.

Examples: `gmail:q:from:(billing@example.com OR accounts@example.com) subject:invoice`, `gmail:q@work:(subject:report OR subject:summary) cc:sam@example.com`, `gmail:q:subject:("quarterly numbers" OR "q3 numbers")`.

**Reading the mailbox.** The reader asks Gmail for the mail added since its last position, page by page (500 records a page, up to 10 pages a poll), and fetches the headers of at most 200 mails a poll; the 200 is checked between history records, never inside one, so a single record that names more than 200 mails is read whole in one poll (it makes that many gets) and the next record waits for the next poll. A busier mailbox is read on from where the poll stopped, straight away. A mail in a mailbox that no query anchor reads, and whose thread no thread anchor watches, is not fetched. A mail is delivered once: the last 1000 mail ids handled are remembered with the reader's position, so a history record that comes back (or names a mail twice) is not delivered twice.

**A restart resumes from the saved position.** The anchors service saves each reader's position (Gmail's history id, Drive's page token) with its other read positions, each minute and at a clean stop, and a start keeps it: mail that arrived, and Drive changes made, while the service was stopped are read from it and delivered once (Drive's changes are read a page of 100 at a time, the next page at once). A save made while a receiver's start is still being retried (the network down at boot) writes back what that receiver loaded, so a second restart before it comes up loses nothing. A position is saved only while an anchor of its kind is bound, so a kind with none saved (never bound, or unbound for a while before the stop) starts from now when an anchor is bound to it, as does a kind that becomes bound after the start. A saved position that Google answers with 404 (Gmail keeps its history for about a week) is replaced by the current one: nothing from before it is delivered, and a failed get saved from before it is dropped with the rest. A saved value of the wrong shape (a number where a text belongs, a list holding other things, a missing field) is ignored for that field and the rest is used.

**A mail whose get fails** is not lost: it is tried again at later polls, 3 tries in all (the failures are saved with the position, 500 at most, each with the time of its last try), and then given up on with a log line, so a mail that Gmail never serves (one deleted in between, say) cannot hold the reader back or be fetched again for ever. A mail's tries are at least 60 seconds apart however many polls come in between, so a short failure of Gmail cannot use them up, and the tried-again gets count against the 200 a poll, the ones over it waiting for the next poll.

## Sent mail watch: a send step ticks itself

A step of the user's that says to send a mail (an item in your-step whose step has a mail word: mail, email, e-mail, Gmail, or the Korean word for mail or email) is ticked by the server when a mail he sent fits it, as his Done ticks it, with no click. A step that names another channel (Slack, KakaoTalk, a text message, a call) is no send step, and neither is one with no mail word: a step to paste a text somewhere else, or to send "the reply" without saying mail. The History and the item's record say the server ticked it on a mail he sent (the answer's `via` reads `mail`), and the answer is no word of his: it carries the server's own sentence and none of his text, it is not an answer to a decision, and it is no word of his for a wait or a date. For delivery too it is no word of his: a mail tick starts no front agent's turn and tells a front agent nothing; a background agent that holds the item is told in plain words what the server did.

**Off until asked.** `[anchor_hub] sent_mail_watch` is `"off"` by default, and only the words `"off"`, `"count"` and `"on"` are values (any case): anything else, `true` and `false` included, reads as off, so a slip never turns a mail reader on. `"count"` is the dry run: the server counts what a mail would have done and ticks, asks and calls nothing. `"on"` does it. It needs a Gmail mailbox the anchors service reads (an account with a token under the secrets folder, as for the Gmail anchors above); nothing else is configured. The user's own addresses are `[anchor_hub] self_emails` and the `gmail_names` addresses. With the setting off and no watch file, the server writes nothing at all.

**What the hub reads.** While the setting is `count` or `on` and at least one send step is open, the server writes `sent-watch.json` in the state folder (the mode, and the threads, addresses and step words of the open send steps), and the anchors service then reads the mail the user sends from the Gmail history it already polls (the SENT label; one `messages.get` for each sent mail, which counts against the same 200 gets a poll). A mailbox Google pushes for its inbox is polled at the `gmail_poll_seconds` pace (300 by default) while the watch is on, since Gmail pushes nothing for sent mail; with the watch off it is left to its push. A sent mail leaves a private file (mode 0600) in `sent-mail/` only when it could fit an open send step: it is on a thread a send step's item anchors, or it went to an address those items name (not a draft, not a mail marked Auto-Submitted or bulk, not a mail to himself alone). With no open send step, or the setting off, it reads no sent mail and costs nothing; a watch file the server has not refreshed for 30 minutes reads as off. The watch is written in the server's minute pass. The reader goes on from the position the anchors service already holds for the mailbox (a Gmail anchor that was reading it keeps its place); a mailbox nothing was reading starts from its position when the watch is first read, so mail sent earlier is not seen. Whatever the position, a mail sent before the step was made never counts: the server drops it.

**What the server does with a mail**, in the order of its cost, for each open send step that was made before the mail left and to somebody besides himself:

| The mail | What happens |
|---|---|
| is on a Gmail thread the item anchors (an anchor scoped to the item) | the step ticks, no model call |
| went to an address the item names (an address in its step, draft, description, subject or next; or an address whose name part is whole words of the step: two words or more, or one word of five letters or more that is no desk name such as info or support) and is on another thread (an edited draft, a new mail) | one call to a Haiku-class model, in a thread of its own, with a time limit: it is given the step, the first 400 characters of the draft and the mail's recipients (their addresses, at most four, never their display names), subject and first words as data (no full bodies); it answers YES, NO or UNSURE |
| fits no open send step | counted and deleted |

A recipient's display name is written by whoever sent him the mail the reply answers, so it never makes a party and is never shown to the model. Only an answer that is exactly YES (any case, one optional full stop) ticks the step; "YES, NO or UNSURE", "YES or NO", "Yes/No", "**YES**" and a YES with a sentence after it are UNSURE. NO leaves the step. An unsure answer, an answer that is none of the three, a call that failed three times (tried again every 5 minutes), and a mail that waited three hours for a call put one plain line on his step, "a sent mail may match; press Done if so", once and never over a status line that is already there; the pick is his.

**A verdict belongs to the step it was asked for.** The call runs in a thread, so the minute pass (wakes, anchors, hand-offs) never waits for it; at most two calls start in a pass, one at a time, and a call that runs past its limit is given up on and its answer, if it ever comes, is thrown away. A second mail to the same party for the same step waits for the next pass. The answer is applied only when the item is still open, still in his step, with the same step text and the same state clock as when the call began; the tick itself checks that again under the item lock. An item that closed, moved on or was given another step during the call gets nothing: no answer, no row, nobody told. An item that also asks him a decision (an ask, options or a decision need) is never ticked by a mail: its needs, ask and options stay exactly as they are, and only the plain line goes on his step. A cap of 6 calls an hour holds (kept in the counts file, so a restart keeps it), and each call is in the router's call log (the item, the model, the cost; never a word of the mail); a mail past the cap waits.

**No mail text is kept.** A mail's file is deleted at its verdict; only a mail waiting for a second try keeps its words, in a private file, until then and for three hours at most, and the anchors service deletes any file older than that itself (with a little grace), whether the server runs or not; a write that fails leaves no temp file. A row holds the recipients (To and Cc, address and the name as sent, at most 20), the subject (120 characters), the first 200 characters of the body as Gmail gives them, and ids and times: never the whole body. `sent-mail-counts.json` in the state folder holds numbers only: `mails`, `thread`, `party`, `unrelated` (by the best fit of each mail), and in mode `on` `judged`, `yes`, `no`, `unsure`, `failed`, `capped`, `ticked`, `asked`, `expired`, and the times of the calls of the last hour. Read it after a few days of `count` before turning `on`.
