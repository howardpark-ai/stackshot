# Doing, waiting and the clock

What this page covers: what doing means, a doing item that only waits for a later day, the goal an item opens with, no clock renewal without progress, on agent versus park, and the blocker a self-set wait must name. Back to the [README](../README.md).

**Doing is work in hand, not a wait, and carries no expected time.** An item in the stored state `doing` shows how long it has run (the item page's "since 2h ago", the home row's age) and nothing else: no expected time is set, kept or shown, and a far time cannot make it read as Waiting on the home page. The board keeps a date only for a wait on an outside party, a date or a person: a wake, a hold, `after`, a relay wait with its `ask_by` day. Work in hand takes `next` and an agent session in `needs`; `expected_by` and `expected_reason`, which older calls pass, are accepted and not stored, and the reply carries one note saying so and naming the park calls. When an `update` leaves an item in the stored state `doing` (not on hold) and a line of `next` it writes reads as a wait on a time or an event ("overnight", "in the morning", "nightly", "the next run", "once the run finishes", "until 09:00", "waiting on the 09:00 timer", `model_guards.reads_as_wait`), the reply carries one note, never a refusal, saying to park it that turn with `update(hold=true, until=…, wait_basis=…)` (a valid reason to wait on an item does not warrant marking it as on agent and letting it sit there for hours: it should be marked waiting). "Until" and "waiting on" count only with a time or a day after them: a builder waiting on its own CI or review, or running tests until green, is work in hand and gets no note. A create takes no state, so it carries none. No expected-by time is derived for any item: the items API still carries `expected_by`, `wait` and `overdue`, always empty, so that a reader written against them does not break, and they go in a later release. A parked item (on hold, or after an open item) carries no stale mark: it is held until its wake or its blocker closes, and a wait that went wrong is caught by the passes that read the item's own fields (the wake pass and the watchers, the dates pass, the failed-check notice, the wait-end audit). A quiet builder is nudged at once: the stall pass does not wait for a date. A doing item stored before this keeps its old `expected_by` on disk, which nothing reads; the one-time pass that parked those whose stored `expected_by` was far ahead (`tools/move_waiting_doing.py`) went with the state inputs, and such an item is parked by hand with `update(hold=true, until=…, wait_basis=…)`. A bare-date wake is the start of the next day, 00:00 in the instance zone.

**A doing item that only waits for a later day reads as waiting** (`stackshot/waits/dated_wait.py`, shown by `model.view`). A `doing` item (stored, or read as doing from its fields when none is stored, as a mover and a wake do; mover a session, not on hold) whose `wake` (one it still carries from before `until`) is more than `[waiting] dated_wait_min_hours` (1) ahead, and whose every open step of `next` names a date (`states.named_dates`: `Mon Mar 2nd`, `Mar 2`, `2099-03-02`) that the step says it waits for (a waiting word stands directly before it, or with one filler word `the`, `a` or `on` between, in the same clause: `after Mar 3`, `until the 2099-03-03`; `after`, `once`, `until`, `till`, `when`, `following`, `wait`; a bracket ends the clause and a waiting word further back never counts, so `merge once CI is green (Mar 3)` and `finish the notes after lunch for the 2099-03-03 release` are no wait; or it opens the step, at the line start or right after the `<name>:` opener, with a waiting word right after it: `Mon Mar 2nd after the run`; never one right after `by`, `due`, `before`, `closed`, `since`, `on`, `eta` or `target`, one followed by `deadline` or `due`, or one behind a comma, colon or dash after other text, as in `deadline: Mar 3` or `Mar 3 - draft the notes`, which are deadlines or references) and that is a later calendar day than today, not before the day of that wake and at most 60 days after it (a yearless date that `named_dates` rolled a year ahead is no wait), is listed as waiting on a time until the wake, a wait on a time: no write, the stored state stays doing, `derive` is unchanged for the guards and the router, and the brief counts these items on a line, the hourly audit line and the idle hand-over skip them (a wake of its own), and `get` and the brief carry the tail `· the board lists it as waiting until <time> (every open step is dated for later (<date>))` on the `model:` line. One open step with no date means the agent can act now: the item stays doing. So does a wake less than the hour away, an item with no open step, and a step dated today or in the past; a step with several dates counts by its earliest; a time of day in a step is not read, the day decides. The daily audit (`stackshot/audit/audit_run.py`) treats such an item as a suspect of the check `why-not-now` (ranked with obsolete-wait alone, after the other checks; at most five a run, inside the `[audit] max_calls` cap; its signature is the dated lines plus the wake) and asks its mover whether something concrete keeps the step from running before that time (an external event, someone else's run, a scheduled job) or the agent could do it now; a finding goes to the mover like the others. A wait has to justify itself. A stored `doing` item that is on hold with a wake more than the hour ahead is listed as waiting on that wake as well (`dated_wait.held_wait`, basis `on hold until its wake`; its steps are not read); a hold with no wake, or a wake already past or within the hour, leaves it as it was. **A wake that carries a `wait_basis` is a wait, hold or not** (an item on hold keeps the reason `on hold until its wake`, with or without a basis) (`dated_wait.basis_wait`): a `doing` item (stored, or read as doing; mover a session; no `wait_kind`, no `needs` of `decision` or `external`, no `after` link; not on hold, which `held_wait` reads) whose wake is still ahead and has a `wait_basis` written for that very wake (`waiting.basis_of`) is listed as waiting on a time until the wake, the basis text as the reason (the item's row and `get` show it, and who set it; this reading is for the item that is not on hold), with no wake floor of an hour and none of its steps read. The basis names what the item waits for ("the contract renews on 2099-01-20"), which a bare wake does not: a wake with no `wait_basis` and no `until` is a backstop and the item stays active. Once the wake has passed the item reads as it is stored again (the wake pass tells its sessions and drops the wake and its basis). It is the same model-layer reading (`model.view`) as the two above, so the home page's lists, the brief, `get`, the wire `list` and the Waiting sort agree; `derive`, the guards and the router still read it as stored, and the daily audit, which asks about dated-step suspects only, does not ask about it. A write that sets such a wake gets none of the notes that a wake alone draws (the window note, the timed-step note), and the brief's advice about doing items with dated steps does not count it.

**An item opens with its goal** (`server._gate_goal`, `bullet_text.first_bullet` and `goal_of`, the audit check `goal-unclear` in `stackshot/audit/audit_run.py`, the report `stackshot/tools/goal_pass.py`; the gate is off with `[description] require_bullets = false`). The subject is the outcome wanted ("Make X work when Y"), never the symptom ("X fails when Y"), and the description's first `- ` bullet opens `Goal:` and says the same outcome in a sentence; a symptom, cause or trigger goes in a later bullet that opens `Problem:`. A session's `create`, and an `update` that replaces the description with text other than what the item already holds, is refused when its first `- ` bullet (a quote line or a fenced block before it is passed over) does not start `Goal:` (case-sensitive, right after the dash, with text after it); a description with no bullet is refused the same way, after the bullet-shape gate has spoken. The refusal names the first six words it saw, says the subject and the first bullet state the outcome, and gives the "Fix X fails when Y" to "Make X work when Y" example. Never judged: an `update` that leaves the description out, a close or `complete`, a subject set alone, scripts (a sender starting `a script on `, the client API's question route, any other script label) and the server's own writes (watchers, post-wake asks, the cold call, the re-bullet route), and the items already on the board. **The audit's `goal-unclear` check** (one model call per suspect, `audit_checks.goal_view`/`goal_sig`, `audit_run.goal_evidence`) asks about an open item that has a `Goal:` first bullet and whose (subject, Goal sentence) pair is not the one the record's `goal_judged` holds for it: does each name an outcome wanted, and do the two agree? Items written for another reason are not asked again; a reworded subject or Goal sentence is; an item with no Goal bullet is not a suspect; at most `GOAL_MAX` (10) a run, ranked with `obsolete-wait` and `why-not-now`, and the cap of calls holds. A yes goes to the item's mover like the other model checks, with the fixed fix text (reword the subject and the first bullet, put the symptom under `Problem:`). **The one-time pass** `stackshot/tools/goal_pass.py [--dry-run] [--max-calls N] [--out FILE] [--store DIR]` asks the same judge about EVERY open item once, the ones with no Goal bullet included, prints id, verdict, reason, subject and first bullet to stdout and to `<paths.state>/audit/goal-pass-<stamp>.txt`, and is report only: it files nothing, sends no message and writes no audit record.

**No clock renewal without progress** (`stackshot/service/gates.py` `gate_clock_renewal`, in the update tool's write path; a session's write only, off with `[model] guards = false`). A write that moves a `wake` or an `ask_by` to a LATER time than the item holds, while the item's reason (the description or the `wait_basis` text; a `wait_reason` does not count, it is not stored) and its open steps (the open lines of `next`) are as they were, is refused: re-dating an item is how work sits unseen. The refusal names the ways out. Change a step in the same write (`done=[…]`, `dropped=[…]`, a new line), state the new reason, or send the owner's own words in `said` (an Acked-by entry checked against his transcript, recent, and a message the item does not hold yet: an unchecked or Reported-by entry, or a message already stored whatever its quote, does not count), or change what the wait stands on to a shape the item never held (`wait_kind`, `wait_event`, `wait_anchor`, `after`; the last few shapes are kept in `metadata.clock_struct`, so a flip back to an earlier one, or removing `after` or `wait_event`, does not count; a `recycle` or `fleet_op` `wait_event` is told by its verb, not its machine argument, and a `pr_*` one by its full target; such a change of shape alone lets a clock push through twice in a row and the third is refused (`metadata.clock_struct_n` counts them; a changed step, the reason, a new owner message or a hand-off resets it); `post_wake_mover` is not part of it: who takes the item after is a hand-off, judged by the holder rules); or, if the item now waits, park it (`hold=true` with `wake` and `wait_basis`) or use the event-wait form (`wait_kind="event"`, `wait_event`, `ask_by`, `post_wake_mover`). The text fits the item: a parked item is told to give a new `wait_basis`, an item on a decision is not told to hold (it cannot be held). Setting a clock for the first time, pulling one in, re-sending the value the item holds, clearing a clock and a real hand-off are not renewals. A hand-off is a write after which the effective holder (the stored `mover`, else the first session in `needs`), resolved through the registry, is another session than before and is not the sender: setting the holder again, flipping it back, or writing the same session as a name and as an id is no hand-off. The third-re-park check on a parked item's wake (`gate_repark`) stays: it counts the pushes a changed step lets through.

**On agent is work moving in real time; a window the work needs is a park** (work moving in real time is 'on agent', however long it takes, and a wait-and-see window the work itself needs is 'waiting' (parked); an item can sit on agent through a long log collection because its mover had only set a wake). A build, tests, a review, a release or a run in progress stays on agent however long it takes. A session parks its own item when the work itself needs the wait: a soak, a log or data collection window, a trial week, a timer it started. The form is `update(hold=true, until="<when the window ends>", wait_basis="<what the window collects> ends <YYYY-MM-DD>")`: the hold with a wake and a `wait_basis` is a park (`states.derive`, `waiting` in the board), it passes `gate_wait_basis` at any length up to a year, and the item shows as waiting until the wake, then returns to its mover. A session does not park to defer work it could do now: an item not on hold is one its mover can move now. A bare date (`until`, or its old name `wake`) is a wait until it and parks the item, except in a write that hands the item on, where it is only the hand-off's backstop: to keep an item moving, set no date. An item the model already reads as a time wait without a hold flag (a verifying item, whose end date is its wait, or any item the model reads as a time wait), or a write that puts the item into the verifying state in the same call, takes `wait_basis` with a closer wake in one call, at any length, as a held item does (`_reads_time_wait`): the `snooze` guard asks that wait for its reason, and the basis is bound to the new wake and satisfies it; `tests/cap_words_scenario.py` section C. When a write sets a wake without hold and the item's next, as it now stands (the lines this call wrote and the stored ones), names that wake's day (`2099-03-02`, `Mar 2nd`, `2 Mar` or `3/2`, each as a whole word; a weekday only right after the waiting word, "wait until Monday") close behind a waiting word (after, once, wait, until, till, collect, soak, "then review": at most three words between, no comma, semicolon or bracket, and none of due, by, before or deadline; after "for N days" the day may follow a comma; `model_guards.reads_as_window`), the reply carries one note, never a refusal, saying the item waits until that date (a date alone is a wait outside a hand-off) and giving the form for a window the work itself needs. **A wake that is the whole of a mover's wait is parked by the write** (`server._date_only_wait`, `dated_wait.timed_wait_lines`, `model_guards.reads_as_timed_step(dated_only=True)`): when the sender is the item's mover and every open step of `next` opens with the wake's own clock or day ("at 08:00", "on <that day>", "on Fri Mar 6th", "on Fri" for the wake's weekday within the week ahead, "Mar 6: …", "tomorrow at 9"; matched at the line's start only, never after a "then", so "build it now, then at 08:00 verify" keeps the note; a day-only step on today itself can run now and is no wait, a clock time on today still counts), with the wake more than `[waiting] dated_wait_min_hours` ahead, no hold or wait_basis in the call and the item open, the write sets `hold` and a `wait_basis` itself (the call's `wait_reason` plus the wake day, else the step's own words) and the usual gates run on that park; the reply says so and names the way out (write the step as the action and pass `clear_hold=true`). A step that starts on an outside event keeps the note, and an action to take now, another party's step, a wake within the hour and a call that holds or gives its own basis are left as they were. "Merge the PR once CI is green; demo on Monday" and "ship after review, due 2099-03-02" draw none. The note is skipped for an item that is not on agent: one on hold, one on the owner's decision, one waiting after an open item, one with `needs=external`, and one stored as outside, verifying or blocked. A new `next` line that reads as a wait ("collect logs until 2099-03-02") is still refused by the `next_wait` guard, whose refusal names the hold, wake and reason, and for a window the work needs the `wait_basis` form above. `stackshot/tools/board_report.py` reads a hold whose stored `wait_basis` carries a real YYYY-MM-DD date as naming its event, so a window park raises no arbitrary-hold finding.

## A wait a session sets on its own item names its blocker

An agent that moves an item can park its own work with a wake, or by dating its own step ("apply the step at Monday's scheduled run"), although nothing stops the step now, and the ready work then sits idle until the date (`stackshot/waits/self_wait.py`). A **self-set wait** is a write by the item's mover (or the creator of an item it moves with `mover=<itself>`) that sets a wake that differs from the stored one (a wake re-sent in another spelling of the same moment, `2099-03-04 10:00` for `2099-03-04T10:00`, sets nothing), or writes a line of its own `next` (a line that opens with its own name) whose step is dated later than today: a date as `states.named_dates` reads it (`2099-03-03`, `Mar 3rd`), `next week`, `next month` or `next weekend` (after a plan cue such as `ship it next week`, after until or till, or opening a clause; `next week's planning doc` is a noun, and the same end-of-phrase rule holds: `what next week looks like` is no wait), and the relative words below, read conservatively because a false refusal stops ordinary work while a missed wait only leaves things as they were: `until Monday` or `next Monday` (a weekday after until, till or next); `on`, `at`, `for`, `after` or `this` with a weekday (`apply it on Monday`) only when the day is the adjunct of a plan cue (apply, ship, retry, re-run, check, will, should, then and the like, at most two words between and no determiner among them: `apply it on Monday`, `retry on Friday`; `check why the release on Monday failed` and `schedule the review on Friday` are no wait; a plain noun after the day makes it a modifier, so `run tests on Monday data` and `look at the Monday failures` are no wait: the phrase must end at the day, at the end of the line, a punctuation mark or a word such as then, and, to, at, so, if, when, before, after, with, for, once, while, as, during, via, per, in, because, since, until; `by` is left out on purpose, since `on Friday by noon` is a deadline) and no reporting or past verb stands near it (`said on Monday`, `found on Tuesday`, `failed on Monday`, `the check on Monday` are no wait); a weekday at the start of a line followed by a doing verb (`Monday: apply the step`); a possessive weekday only after a preposition and before a run-like noun (`at Monday's scheduled run`; `Monday's update`, `Monday's build` and `Monday's log` are no wait); `tomorrow` or `tonight` as a word that ends the clause or is followed by then, and, at, morning and the like (not `tomorrow.sh`, not `the tomorrow parser`, not `tomorrow's agenda`); `at`, `on`, `for`, `until`, `after` or `with` before `the next scheduled`, `nightly`, `daily` or `weekly run` (`document how the next nightly run works` is no wait); `after the weekend` after a plan cue or opening a clause, and `until the weekend`; `in 3 days` or `in two weeks` opening a clause (`in 3 days check the build`) or after a plan cue and at the end of the clause (`re-check in 3 days`; `the build finished in 2 days` and `the retry in 3 days case` are no wait). A date is a **deadline** and never a wait when it follows by, due, before, deadline, target, ETA, "no later than", "prior to", "ahead of" or "aim for" (a colon, a dash, a comma or up to eight words may sit between: `ship it, due: Mar 9`, `by the end of the day on Mar 9th`), or is followed by deadline, due, "or earlier" or "at the latest" (`Mar 9 at the latest`); a word that is a wait itself (until, till, after) ends that gap. A wake in no valid form, or not in the future, is left to the write's own wake check: it gets that refusal and no model is asked. Such a write carries `wait_reason=<what the step waits on that is not available now>` (at least `15` characters; the server does not store it): without it the write is refused, and the refusal says what to add. With a reason, one model call (`router.run_call`: the `[router]` command and lane, no tools, safe mode; `[wait_guard] model` picks the model, `timeout_seconds` the time) is asked whether the step could be done now, given the reason; an answer of yes refuses the write and quotes the model's reason, saying to do it now or name the real blocker. The call fails open: no model, a timeout, an error or an unreadable answer lets the write through, and so does a call past `[wait_guard] max_calls` (30 in any rolling hour); each is one line on stderr (`wait guard: …`). The check runs before the item lock is taken (it reads the item, writes nothing), so a slow call never holds the board. **Not judged**, because the wait already names its blocker: a write that leaves the item waiting on another item (`after`), on the owner or an outside party (`needs` decision or external), on a stored `wait_kind`, a hold (a park carries its `wait_basis`), an item that is not alive (one that already waits by its own state: parked, outside, blocked, verifying, or on the owner's call), an item whose mover is another session or the owner, a `next` that hands the work to another session (`<session>: <action>`), a step the item already carried, and a close. `[wait_guard] enabled = false` turns the whole check off (default on). The reasons, the model's answer and the cap are in `stackshot/waits/self_wait.py`'s docstring; `tests/self_wait_scenario.py` replays the case with a stubbed model.

**A wait is one stored date, `until`** (`stackshot/waits/until_write.py`, `update_unlocked` in `stackshot/service/update.py`; `tests/until_write_scenario.py`). An open item with an `until` (YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM) is waiting; `wait_kind` (`time`, `anchor`, `item`, `relay`, `event`) only stands beside it, and the item's `status` is unchanged. The `wake`, `ask_by` and a hold's date are one date now: a write that passes one of them (`wake`, `ask_by`, `hold`) is still accepted for 14 days after Wed Nov 11th 2026 (`[model] wait_alias_until`, default 2026-11-25), stores `until` and none of the old fields, and says so in its reply; after that day it is refused with the name of `until`. A kind with no date is never refused: the date comes from the item's own words (an absolute date, tomorrow, a weekday), else from its deadline or the item it waits after, else it is tomorrow, and the reply says which. The server clears `until` and `wait_kind` when the wait ends; `due` stays a separate field. A hold that waits on an item, a recycle or an outside party and has no date of its own keeps its flag. A date with no kind, hold, basis or after link is a wait on that date alone outside a hand-off; in a write that hands the item on to a session (a `mover` argument, a session named in `needs`, a hand-off line in `next`, a reopen with one) it is only the hand-off's backstop: it is stored as the `wake` (or `ask_by`) it was given as, the item stays active in the new mover's lane, the hand-off record is made, and the reply says so (`server._hands_on`, which reads the mover the write leaves, as the hand-off record does; `tests/handoff_scenario.py` 15.1b and 15.1c). An item whose needs hold his decision is never parked by a date (`server._date_parked` and `_is_parked` are one rule).
