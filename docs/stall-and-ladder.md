# The silence pass, the stall pass and the ladder

What this page covers: the silence pass, the stall pass, the one ladder of nudges, and how the hand-off record follows the mover. Back to the [README](../README.md).

**The silence pass** (`silence.py`, `server._silence_tick`, a ten-minute entry after the dates pass; it needs `[audit] enabled`, script mode). It replaces the daily run's two silence rules, the stale check's 12 hours without a write on an item with no date of its own and the board report's idle-on-agent (6 hours), with one clock on the item: `activity.at`, the time of its last real activity (a step done or added, a state change, a new next step or description, the owner's answer, a message written onto the item, an event on one of its anchors). An audit's own write and a bare confirm do not move it, so the pass's message cannot reset the clock it reads. An open item in the state doing, on a session, with no activity for 45 minutes (`silence.LIMIT`) is found; an item on hold, behind an open item, with a stored wait (a release, a CI run, any event wait, a relay, an outside party), with a wake of its own (a wake ahead is its expected time; one that passed is the dates pass's; but a wake never silences a first open step that is already overdue and names no date of its own, a step the front-step rule moved or found stuck: `front_step.overdue`) or with a hand-off nobody took yet (the hand-off audit's alone) is left alone. The count starts at the later of that activity and the moment the item left a hold, a wait or an after link (a wake, a lifted hold and a released after link are saved by the server with no writer, so the activity clock does not move for them): an item parked for a day is not found the round it wakes. Silence is judged per item, never per pane: the pass does not read the mover's pane, so a busy pane (a builder directing subagents is nearly always busy), a background agent's `shell` pane or a pane this box does not show hides no silent item. Before it sends, the pass asks one view (`server._silence_skip`): an item the stall ladder is already on (`_stall_active`) is left alone, and a view that cannot be built sends nothing (fail open). A subagent's work that writes nothing to the item is not activity, so a builder keeps a long job visible by writing it onto the item; one such write is read beyond the clock: an item whose mover is its project's builder and which carries a `work` line in progress (changed within 90 minutes, `silence.subagent_running`; a job that runs longer changes the line's words or writes to the item, since a line sent again unchanged keeps its old time) has a build subagent running on it and counts as taken, while a builder's item with no such line and no event in 45 minutes is silent whatever its pane shows. A finding is the daily run's `stale` finding in its wording ("no write for N minutes and no date of its own", the same fix; a span past four hours reads in hours and minutes), so the two share one `finding_key`; it goes to the mover (else the project's -main agent) through `deliver_findings`, once per silence episode: an episode ends when the item has activity again, and the send stamp is compared with the item's activity, not with its last write. The pass's record is `<state>/audit/silence.json` (`started`, `findings` with their send stamps, `carried`: the stamps of findings that went away with no activity on their item, so a wait filed and lifted does not restart the episode); like the filing and dates passes it does nothing while a daily run is in progress, and the stall pass stays (it watches the pane of a front agent or a session no page lists as a background agent: a session with no turn running, the ladder to the cold call and the builder; a background agent's rungs after the ask go by the item's own work line, see **The one ladder**; with `[ladder] silence` on it takes the rungs of the items this pass brings instead of starting its own). By default it sends the ask rung only, as one message per silence episode; with `[ladder] enabled` and `[ladder] silence` both true that message is the ask of the one ladder and the item goes on it (below). Suites: `tests/ladder_silence_scenario.py`, `tests/silence_scenario.py`, `tests/runner_scenario.py`, `tests/runner_order_scenario.py`.

**The stall pass** (`stall.py`, `server._stall_tick`). Every `[stall] every_seconds` (20; the stall pass runs on the runner's 60-second pace, so a smaller value still runs once per round) it looks at every open, live item whose mover is a session, front agents included. **The signal** is the CLI's registry (`~/.claude/sessions/<pid>.json`, read by `server._handoff_status`, the same read the hand-off clock uses): the mover's live process has `status: idle` (or `shell`, which the CLI writes for a pane whose turn is over but which has a `run_in_background` Bash task running; a background subagent keeps the pane `busy`, which is left alone) and its `statusUpdatedAt` is `[stall] minutes` (10) old or more. A pane that starts a turn goes busy and, back to idle, has a later `statusUpdatedAt`, so a turn starting restarts the count; an item that only just reached its mover counts from then, not from the pane's age. A pane the registry does not show on the server's box (another machine's, a dead process) says nothing, and its items are left alone. **The ladder**, each step `minutes` after the one before it, counted from the later of the pane going idle, the item reaching its mover and the previous step: (1) the notice goes to the mover once more (one message per mover, items batched; **a front agent is sent none**, because no item notice wakes a front agent: its step 1 is the History line alone, and steps 2 and 3 cover its item; at most `[stall] max_per_tick` (5) first steps a tick, the rest on the next ticks (the cap is per tick, so with one tick a minute a burst of 30 idle panes after a deploy takes about 6 minutes); **no clock starts before the pass first ran**, recorded under `_pass` in `<state>/stall.json`, so a deploy or a restart does not step every long-idle pane at once); (2) the hand-off's cold call (`server._handoff_cold`, through the same writer, [model] `cold_call`) names who takes it, at most once per item in 24 hours (**a stall cold call never asks the owner**: the record's `stall` marker gives it `cold_call.STALL_RUBRIC`, which offers `hand_to` and `close` only and answers `none` when unsure; an `ask_owner` answer, a `none`, an answer it could not give or a write the item refused is not written, so no `needs`, ask or mover changes, a `stall:` journal line and a History line say "the cold call named no taker: to the builder", and the item goes on to step 3 at once; the ordinary hand-off cold call keeps its three answers); (3) the project's builder (`server._handoff_builder`, through `_handoff_exec`, which reads the builder as section 5 does); (4) when none of them can take it, the last rung: a History line ("stackshot: …") and a journal line, and ONE message to the fleet's builder (`[fleet] operator_session`, `server._recovery_tell`), first line `[stackshot] fleet recovery: <item> …`, naming the item, its mover, why and what was tried, and asking it to take the fix through its usual path. The hand-off record carries it (`recovery_at`), so the pass sends it once, and the mark is written before the send on purpose (a failed send is not retried every pass: `metadata.undelivered` records it and the audit's unmoved digest is the backstop); the History line is written after the send and says what happened (asked, or why nothing was sent); a write by the item's mover, or the item moving on, ends the record and a later episode may send another. The item stays with its mover and does not go to the owner; no list of his carries it. A stall record (`metadata.handoff.stall`) runs no clock of the hand-off ladder (`no_clock`) and never returns the item to a sender. **Not looked at:** an item on hold, on a wait or moved by the owner or an outsider (the model derives no session mover for them), a hand-off in flight to a session that is not a front agent (the hand-off ladder, `[model] handoff_minutes`, owns it), a cold call running, **an item whose work is not due now, whatever its mover's role** (`stall.not_due`, read from the item model's own fields: a `wake` (check-back) still ahead, an `expected_by` still ahead (an idle pane is not a stalled one: a builder that ended its turn while a background task runs shows `shell`, which counts as idle once the item is due, and one with a background subagent running shows `busy`; an item past its expected-by shows as past due in get and on the board), a hold or a park, an `after` link to an item still open, a wait on the owner (`needs` decision, his step) or on someone outside (`needs` external). Such an item gets no step and **no record is kept for it**, so a leftover ladder position does not carry over: when it falls due (the wake or expected-by passes, the hold lifts, the answer comes) its clock starts then, the mover's idle time counted from the later of the pane going idle and that moment), an item already raised in the last 24 hours, and an item whose pane status is unknown. Every step is written on the item (`metadata.stall.log`, one History line each, "stackshot: …"; the last three show in `get` as `stall:` lines) and a `stall:` line in the journal; the episode's progress is in `<state>/stall.json` (an entry restarts when anyone writes the item or its mover changes). It needs `[model] handoff` on and not paused. **A pause of the pass always ends on its own** (a pause that is forgotten would otherwise leave idle builders asleep). Two keys pause it, both read live on a config reload: `[stall] paused_until` (an ISO-8601 time with an offset, such as `"2099-03-14T21:00+00:00"`) holds the pass until then and it runs again with no edit; `[stall] enabled = false` holds it for at most `[stall] max_pause_hours` (default 4) counted from when the server first saw the pause (the reload or start that read it; the start time is kept in memory and in `<state>/stall_pause.json`, so a restart does not reset the clock). The cap covers a `paused_until` too: one further out than `max_pause_hours` from when it was first seen is cut to the cap, and a changed setting is a new pause seen at that moment. After the end the pass runs although the file still says `enabled = false`; edit the file to start another pause. The journal has one line when a pause starts, `stall: paused until Fri Mar 14th 2098 21:00 (paused_until)` (the reason is `paused_until`, `enabled=false, capped at 4 h` or `paused_until, capped at 4 h`), and one when it ends, `stall: pause ended, the pass runs again`, which is also a line on the routing log (ruled `dropped` by `stackshot (auto)`, under the page of the fleet operator's project, `[fleet] operator_session`). A reload logs `config reload: warning: the stall pass is paused and takes no step until …`, and `./stackshot doctor` and the server's start print a `[stall] the stall pass is paused …` note with the end time. **An agent never switches the pass off**. The server honors `enabled = false` or a `paused_until` still ahead only when `[stall] pause_item` names a board item that carries the owner's own answer approving a pause (`metadata.answers`, written only by his board login or his GitHub comment, never by a session's `update`; the chosen option or the comment says "pause", and an unrelated answer of his does not count) dated no earlier than 4 hours before the server first saw the setting (an answer that comes only after the pause's cap has passed starts it then, with a new clock and one `stall: paused until … (started late …)` line); the cap on any pause is at most 24 hours (`max_pause_hours` above 24 is read as 24). Any other setting is refused: the pass keeps running, the journal has one line, `stall: pause refused, the pass keeps running: <why>` (once per config load, not per tick; a reload or a restart logs it again once), and `./stackshot doctor`, the server's start and a reload print the same reason. **When the pass takes a wrong step on an item**, an agent (1) undoes the step on the item with `update`: a hand-off (`cold`, `builder`) is undone by setting `mover` and `next` back, a close by `status=open` with a new `next`, a recovery request needs nothing undone (the message to the fleet's builder cannot be unsent; the item stayed with its mover); the `stall:` line in the item's History stays as the record; a notice already sent (`resend`) cannot be unsent and needs nothing; (2) fixes the cause through a normal PR to stackshot (`not_due`, `action` or the registry signal read the item wrong, which is code), handed to my-engineer; (3) if wrong steps reach the owner before the fix lands, asks him for a pause that ends by itself: he answers on a board item, and `pause_item` names it. The same text is in `stall.py`'s docstring and in the `get` tool (the `update` tool has a short clause pointing to it).

**The one ladder** (`ladder.py`, `server._ladder_*`, `[ladder] enabled`, off by default). The hand-off ladder (a reminder for a background agent, the project's builder, the cold call, the sender, the fleet's builder asked) and the stall ladder (the notice again, the cold call, the builder, the fleet's builder asked) were two orders on two records, and an item could climb both. With `[ladder] enabled = true` an item whose mover went quiet has one record, its entry in `<state>/stall.json` (its `via` says which audit brought it: `handoff` for a hand-off nobody took, `stall` for a pane with no turn running), and one order: (1) **ask**: the mover is sent the notice again (a hand-off's own notice for an untaken hand-off, the stall notice for a silent pane; a front agent gets the History line alone); (2) **look**: `[ladder] look_minutes` (30) after the ask, and only then, the pane is looked at: with no turn running the notice goes again, with a turn running nothing is sent and the look comes when the turn ends (a background agent's pane is not looked at, see **Background movers** below); (3) **cold**: `[stall] minutes` (10) later the cold call names who takes it (it never asks the owner); (4) **builder**: `[stall] minutes` later, or at once when the cold call named no taker, the project's builder takes the item; with none able, ONE message asks the fleet's builder to recover the session, the item stays with its mover and is not put to the owner. The two audits that bring an item stay apart: the hand-off tick (`_handoff_tick`) brings an untaken hand-off (not taken in `[model] handoff_minutes`, a turn that ended with no write, a gone pane, two failed pushes: those last two start at the cold call, there being nothing to ask), the stall tick (`_stall_tick`) a silent pane, and both take the rungs after the first here. An untaken hand-off to a session is the hand-off audit's alone (the stall tick does not start it) and the hand-off tick leaves an item already on the ladder, so one item never climbs two ladders at once. The sender rung is gone (an untaken hand-off is not sent back to the session that handed it over), and so is the reminder as a step of its own: a background agent is asked like any mover, so one whose turn ended with no write is now asked after the idle grace period rather than after the `handoff_minutes` clock, and the reminder's extra wait is gone. A write to the item by anyone but the mover, or the mover's own confirm (at most `stall.RESTARTS` times), restarts the episode but keeps the item on the ladder: the ask is not sent a second time, the look comes `look_minutes` after the last rung taken, and the hand-off tick does not bring the item again; the mover starting work on the item takes the hand-off and ends the ladder, as does a new mover, and the entry is for the hand-off it was made for (`ho_at`: a later hand-off of the item to the same mover starts a ladder of its own). The rungs after the ask are taken by the stall tick, so while the stall pass is paused (`[stall]` paused, or `[model] handoff` off) the ladder waits where it is and goes on when the pass runs again; the cold call is made at most once per item in 24 hours. With the flag off both ladders run as they did; turning it off in the middle of a ladder hands the item back to them, the stall ladder reading the ladder's steps in its own shorter table (`ladder.unwind`: a builder rung still to come stays reachable). The idle pass leaves an item the ladder has a step on for the whole ladder (`_stall_active`). **Background movers** (`ladder.action(background=, live=)`, `server._stall_tick`). A background agent's pane is nearly always busy or showing a background shell (a CI watch, a release), and that says nothing about one item, so with `[ladder] enabled` the rungs after the ask (the look, the cold call, the builder) of an item whose mover is a background agent wait on the clock alone, never on its pane, and a background shell no longer exempts a builder. The evidence that the mover is working on the item is the item's own work line: a `work` line in progress, whose agent is the mover itself (another agent's stale line holds nothing), changed within 90 minutes (`silence.subagent_running`) holds the ladder where it is, and the rungs go on 90 minutes after the line was last changed. Three limits: only a background agent (a front agent, and a session no page lists as a background agent, keep the pane rule); one notice per background agent per stall pass (its other items wait for the next pass); and a background agent whose pane is live (the registry shows it, or the heartbeat holds it running; a heartbeat that is empty or unreadable is unknown, and counts as live) stops at the look, since the cold call and the builder replace a mover that cannot answer and a live one can: they are taken only when its pane is not live (or the hand-off audit entered the item as gone: two failed pushes, no delivery, whatever the heartbeat shows), and a builder is never handed its own item (the last rung is then the one message to the fleet's builder). The ask of a ladder a pane with no turn running brought (`via` = `stall`) still waits for that pane. **The silence switch** (`[ladder] silence`, off by default, and it does nothing unless `[ladder] enabled` is true as well; only a TOML true turns it on, read at use): the ladder has a third way in, and the pane with no turn running stops being one. With it on, the ten-minute silence pass (an open item on an agent with no activity for 45 minutes) and the dates pass (an item past its own expected time with no write since) send their message to the mover as before, and for each fresh finding that reached someone (`silence_pass(entered=)`, `audit_run.dates_pass(entered=)`, `server._ladder_enter_quiet`) the item goes on the ladder with `via` = `silence`: that message is the ask, so the entry starts on the look, with its clock started when the message went and a History line ("asked for news"). The look comes `look_minutes` (30) later, and only if the mover's pane has no turn running (a turn running waits; a front agent gets the History line alone; a background agent's pane is not read, see **Background movers** below); the mover is told once more in the silence wording ("nothing written for N minutes", N from the item's last write; `server._ladder_quiet_look`), then the cold call `[stall] minutes` after the look, then the project's builder, then ONE message to the fleet's builder, the item staying with its mover and off the owner's list, as above. The entry is for this silence alone: it ends when the item has activity (its `activity` clock moves past the entry's `act`) or its mover changes, so a bare confirm by the mover and a write that is no activity restart nothing (the stall pass's `RESTARTS` do not apply to it), and a leftover entry of the cold call's 24-hour bound is kept. A pane with no turn running no longer starts a ladder (`_stall_tick` only takes the rungs of an entry the silence audit or the hand-off audit made; an untaken hand-off is still the hand-off audit's). A finding sent before the switch was turned on is not entered (no flood: such an item climbs from its next silence). Turning `silence` off (with `enabled` on) lets an entry already made finish its rungs and brings the pane trigger back; turning `enabled` off reads the entry in the old stall table (the look maps to the cold call). Suite: `tests/ladder_silence_scenario.py`.

**The hand-off record follows the mover** (`handoff.follow`, `server._handoff_follow`, called by `_save` on every save). An item has one open hand-off record (`metadata.handoff`), and it never names someone but the item's effective mover: a session's write hands over through `handoff.apply` as before; any other save ends a record that names a mover the item left (it goes to `handoff_history` as "handed on, not taken", or "the item no longer has a mover"), and a save of the server's own that changes the mover to a session (a wake that makes the post-wake mover the mover, an event wake, the stall pass, a release of `after`) records the hand-off to the new mover, from the server, marked `no_clock`: **the 10-minute take clock still starts only on a session's hand-off**, never on a server-made record, and the timing of a woken mover stays with the stall pass, which leaves a busy pane alone. A taken record to the same name does not count as already naming the mover: a wake that hands the item back to a session that took it before makes a new record (the taken one goes to the history as "handed on"). The record is marked delivered only when a notice actually reaches the mover: a record to a front agent, one whose wake notice is held, and one whose notice goes to a session in needs other than the mover stay undelivered. The owner's answer, a script's save and a hand-back out of the owner's state end a stale record and make none; a record that carries a recovery request (the ladder's last rung) of an open item is left, unless a save of the server's own moves the item on to another session: that ends it as "handed on" and records the new mover (so the stall pass is not silent for it); the open record of a closed item is ended. Delivery is recorded on every notice path: the message tool, the router's push, the keeper's page-owed batch and the anchors prompt mark the open record of an item the text carries the notice link of to that session, and the message tool one the text cites (`server._handoff_told_text`; a batch that only cites another item in a subject marks nothing for it), so a lost notice can be told from a fast take. Records the older code left are repaired by a pass in the server's start (`server._handoff_repair_pass`, after the mover migrations and before the wake pass and any thread; idempotent, one log line with the count): it ends each open record that names someone but the mover and makes nothing new. `tools/handoff_repair.py --store <copy of the store>` is the rehearsal (a dry run unless `--apply`); `--live` is a read-only dry run, and `--live --apply` is refused while the user unit `stackshot.service` is active.

**A hand-off is taken only by work started** (`handoff.started`, `handoff.touch`, called by `_save` through `handoff.apply`). The mover's write takes its open hand-off only when the write shows work begun: a step marked done (the checklist's own mark), a work line (`update work={…}`) new or moved to `in progress` or `done`, or the item closed. An anchor scoped to the item added since the hand-off counts through `plan` as before. A note, a confirm, a reword of `next` or the description, a queue or wait line, a hold or wake, a dropped or new step and a timeline line do not, so they leave the 10-minute clock and its reminders running; the sentence of a note is never read, only the marks. The hand-off notices tell the mover so.

**A background agent that does not take a hand-off is reminded first** (with `[ladder] enabled` off only: on the one ladder there is no separate reminder, see above) (`handoff.plan`, `handoff.remind`, `server._handoff_exec`). When a hand-off to a background agent is due and not taken (delivered and `[model] handoff_minutes` old; for such an agent a turn that ended with no write does not hurry it, the clock alone does), the first step is one reminder to that agent's own session, through the normal push: the item was handed over, no work on it has started, start its subagent alongside the current work. The recipient is checked fail closed (`server._handoff_reminder_target`): the name must resolve to a live session that the role map lists with a background role and not retired, so a front agent, a page's name-line session, an unknown name, a keeper entry, or an unreadable role map gets no reminder and the ladder is what it was. The record gets `reminded_at`, its delivery is forgotten and the clock restarts from the reminder's own push result (delivered, or queued plus the inbox grace); a reminder whose push fails twice goes down the ladder. After a reminder only the clock counts (not the turn-end rule): when the item is still not taken after another `handoff_minutes`, section 5 goes on as before (the project's builder, the cold call, the sender, then the recovery request to the fleet's builder). Several reminders due in one pass for one agent go in one message. A new hand-off of the same item is a new record and is reminded again. An anchor scoped to the item and added at or after the hand-off counts as taken, for every mover (a pull request in flight is a build started; an older anchor says nothing about this hand-off): such an item is neither reminded nor moved on; the anchors are read once per pass and only for an item that would act. A `no_clock` record (one the server made) and a front agent stay without a clock.
